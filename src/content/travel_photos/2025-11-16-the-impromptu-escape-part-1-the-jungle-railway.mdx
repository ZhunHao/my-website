Teaching week finally wrapped up, and with three weeks of freedom before exams, I booked a ticket and left. No real itinerary—just a plan to ride the rails all the way from Johor Bahru to Bangkok, at least until the military checkpoints across the Thai border forced a change of plans.

The first leg started at JB Sentral, where I boarded the 20:25 overnight Jungle Railway north toward Tumpat.

To break up the journey, I hopped off in the quiet town of Kuala Lipis and rested there for a night, passing towering limestone cliffs along the tracks and wandering through string-lit alleys before catching the train north toward the border at Rantau Panjang the next day.

I originally planned to stay on the rails all the way through Thailand, but crossing the border at Rantau Panjang into Narathiwat delivered a quick reality check. Armed soldiers in full camouflage stood guard on the streets, and military checkpoints appeared every few kilometers. Relying on the southern train line to reach Bangkok safely suddenly felt like pressing my luck.

Navigating through the bustling street market with a mix of broken English and hand signs, I tracked down a local minivan to the airport, traded the slow railway for a domestic flight out of Narathiwat, and touched down at Don Mueang by evening.
